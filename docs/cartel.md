# Cartel (#437)

Design grilled with the owner on 2026-11-27. Related: #366 (gang at the marihuana patch), #398 (bus jobs), #436 (ambulance jobs), #358 (bots in vehicles). Built in #494 to #497 and the drops rework (#437); see each section.

## Terms (in `GLOSSARY.md`)

- **Cartel**: the drug cartel a player can work for. Opt-in.
- **Cartel rank**: a player's position, a personal ladder: Runner, Dealer, Sicario, Capo, Patrón (names, curve in `cartel.json`).
- **Respect**: the cartel's own XP; the only thing a job pays.
- **Contact**: the cartel NPC at the plantation; where you join, quit and start jobs.
- **Job**: one task from a giver, with an objective, a reward and a failure. The generic core; the cartel is its first job line (#398 and #436 reuse it later). Not an Event.
- **Heist**: a Payday-like Job: bag the goods, load the van, drive it to a drop point, under Assaults.
- **Duffel**: a carried bag of goods (weed, cash, product). Not the Bag (the six-cell inventory) and not Loot (the Thieves' stolen things).
- **Cartel van**: the drivable getaway van of a heist.
- **Drop point**: where a Delivery's Duffel is handed over (zone kind `cartel_drop`). A Heist's drop is a Receiver.
- **Receiver**: a Heist's drop: a stash house, a helicopter or a boat that takes the Duffels (see "Receivers").
- **Stash house**, **Landing spot**, **Mooring**: the three kinds' places.
- **Rival crew**: the gang that sends the Assaults and holds trap houses.
- **Assault**: one Rival crew van, driven like a chase car, that attacks the Cartel van at a heist's objective and drop.
- **Chase car**: a Rival car (a Rival driver, the melee, and 3 Gunmen) that meets (put down ahead of it on its way to the drop) and follows the loaded Cartel van over the roads and open ground.
- **Gunman**: a new Gang member kind that fights at range.
- **Trap house**: a hollow house a rival crew holds for a Trap house heist.
- **Rival dealer**: the target of a Hit.

## Membership and rank

- Opt-in at the Contact. Quitting is allowed; rank and Respect stay frozen until a rejoin.
- Rank and Respect persist on the Profile (ADR 0003 store, a migration in both SQLite and Postgres).
- Bots never join. A guest (no stored Profile) cannot join. A sandbox player can run jobs and earns nothing.
- Rank-up: a toast to everyone (no chat line: only players' messages are broadcast, #499).
- Rank unlocks: postponed (see v2).

## Who runs a job

- **ffa**: solo only. Squads stay team-deathmatch only.
- **tdm**: the squad. Squads gain a **leader**: the creator; when the leader leaves, the longest-standing member takes over, and the running job goes with them. The leader is marked in the Squads screen and on the scoreboard. A player in no squad runs jobs alone.
- Only the leader starts a job. The reward goes to every squad member at the moment of success, wherever they are (a member who joined midway gets it, one who left does not).
- A squad mate who is not in the cartel shares the objective and can help, earns nothing, and gets the hint "Join the cartel at the Contact" (a guest, who cannot join, reads why instead: no saved profile). Joining midway turns the hint off at once (the Job message goes again when a member's flags change).
- A player is in one party at most: a solo player who joins a squad leaves their solo job (it fails, nobody left to run it).
- One running job per squad (or solo player). Many jobs can run at once, beside any Event. Two jobs never share a drop point, a target spot or a house.
- Only the job's players see its markers and tracker. Everyone sees the duffels on carriers' bodies. Outsiders can interfere by shooting; they get nothing special.

## Difficulty and reward

- **Tier**: the Contact lists jobs in tiers; the leader's rank unlocks the higher ones. Higher tier: more guards, tighter timers, more Respect.
- **Squad size** scales enemy counts, duffel counts and so on (everyone gets the full reward).
- Reward depends on tier only. Respect only: no global XP, and no XP for killing job NPCs (no `GangKill`).
- Heists: base Respect plus a bonus per loaded duffel above the minimum, times the tier. A failed job pays nothing.

## Failure

- A job fails when its timer runs out (a safety for heists, the pressure for simple jobs), when the round ends (at the end screen, not at the next round) or resets, or when the leader quits the cartel.
- Death fails it: solo, the player's death; in a squad, the **leader's** death (other members respawn and carry on; a carried duffel drops where they fell).
- Heists also fail when the Cartel van is destroyed.
- No Respect penalty, no cooldown. A job's NPCs, duffels and van are removed when it ends.
- An admin's `/cartel end fail` fails it too ("Ended by an admin", `JobFailReason.Admin`): a test, see "Testing".

## Job done
- When a Job succeeds, each player of its party gets a short cinematic of about 10 s, on their own client only (the Job's end message already goes to the party alone; no wire change). It begins with an original banner in large text for about 4 s (`banner_seconds`): "Job done" and under it "+N Respect" (what that player was paid: `CartelJobMsg.RespectPaid`). A guest, a non-member or sandbox play earns none: the line says why (`CartelWords.NoRespect`) instead; a member paid 0 sees no second line. A Job is announced once.
- The camera leaves the body in episodes: a quick eased move (about 0.35 s), a soft white flash (about 0.16 s, peak opacity 0.5), then a slow drift (a slight push in and turn) while it holds: (a) behind and above the player (6 m back, 3 m up, 1.5 s), (b) 25 m up looking down at the party (2.5 s), (c) 80 m up, the squad small below (4.5 s); then a flashed cut (0.05 s) straight back into the player's own view at about 9.6 s. The camera only ever zooms out; no sound goes with the moves. The party's centre is the player and their living squad mates within 30 m (the player alone with none), followed smoothly. The camera never goes through terrain or a roof: a wall in the way brings the low view in, any view is put 2 m above the highest surface over it. The own figure is drawn meanwhile; the weapon in the hand and the crosshair are hidden.
- Controls: during the cinematic the own body's movement and weapon inputs are not sent (it stands; the view's heading is held, so the mouse turns nothing). In a road vehicle the camera stages start from the vehicle (its chase or seat camera is the "eye"); a driver's input becomes the brake (the car stops), a passenger just watches. A press of jump, fire, interact or escape (the menu key) skips to the end at once (the view is the player's again, a quick flash covers the cut); damage taken or death does the same. The controls come back 0.3 s after a skip (so the key that skipped does nothing else).
- Music: the project owner's own track *Grove After Dark* v4 (`assets/grove-after-dark/`, Ogg Vorbis from the owner's wav; the owner's own work, not CC0: see its `LICENSE.txt`), sounds.json `job.done.music`, Music bus. It starts with the cinematic, plays under it, fades out over its last 3 s (9 to 12 s from the start, so it ends two seconds after the camera is back) and stops; a skip fades it out over 1.5 s. The old SAX07 jingle is gone (file, sounds.json entry and licence line).
- Flashes (photosensitivity, `docs/standards/ui-ux.md`): short, soft (peak at most 0.6), at most 3 a second (the legs are checked on load: a move and its hold are at least a third of a second; a skip's own flash waits for a third of a second after a leg's flash). None with reduce motion.
- Reduce motion (the setting), or a seat the camera cannot film (an aircraft, a turret, the missile, dead): **banner and music only** (the banner without its fade; the music over 4 s, fading from 2.5 s). No camera, no flash, no lock: the controls stay the player's. (Chosen over a slow fade to a high view: any big change of view is what the setting is for.)
- Numbers: cartel.json `job_done_cinematic` (stages, legs, flash, music, skip, reduced; first guesses, nothing played). Built: `ShooterCore/Cartel/JobDone.cs` (machine `Cartel.JobDone`: Idle, Playing, Skipped, Fading; the banner's detail, skip, damage, flash and music level), `ShooterCore/Cartel/JobDoneCinematic.cs` (the data, `JobDoneTimeline`: the stage at a time, the flash, the music; `CinematicRules.PartyCentre`; unit-tested in `JobDoneCinematicTests`), `CartelClient.Done`, `HeadsUp/Prompts/JobDoneBanner.cs`, `HeadsUp/Vision/FlashVeil.cs`, `Net/Client/OwnPlayer/JobDoneClient.cs` (the camera: the one composer's last word on foot in `DeathView`, over the chase or seat camera in a vehicle; the music voice; the input filter) and `OwnFigure.Seen`. Not run or screenshot-checked: the Godot side was built only.
- Not done: a brief desaturation or blur with the flash (not cheap here: it needs a shader that reads the screen; the flash alone carries the effect); the camera is not placed in an aircraft, at a turret or when dead.

## Contact

- A placed object (position and facing) set by hand in the map editor near the plantation. A map without one has no cartel, with no error.
- Cannot be hurt: no weapon is tested against him (as a Companion).
- Stands alone in v1. #366 later puts the patch gang round him as guards: they never start a fight and fight back only at whoever attacks them (the Gang member rule), member or not.
- E opens a non-pausing panel (as the Hunter's Log): join / quit, rank and Respect bar, the job list by tier (locked tiers show the rank they need), Start (leader only; others see "Your leader starts jobs"). UI follows `docs/standards/ui-ux.md` and the `ui-work` skill.
- During a job: a HUD tracker (objective line, timer) and a marker on the minimap and in the world.
- Built (#494, round 2): the server sends a Job's party its objective line and marker (`IJobObjective.Objective` / `Marker`) as event 37 `CartelObjective` when either changes (the marker when it moved `marker_resend_m`) and again to all after a party change. The Contact's key is `KeyCompetitor.Contact` (his reach keeps the plantation's hold off it); the client gives him the key after every other prompt (`CartelClient`), and his panel is `Cartel/ContactPanel.cs` over `ContactPanelModel` (ShooterCore). The tracker is `CartelTracker` (machine `Cartel.Tracker`), drawn by `HeadsUp/Prompts/JobTracker.cs`; the marker is `MapMarkers.Job` (a star, `MapGlyphs.Job`, `Maps/JobMarker.cs`). A rank-up (event 36) is a toast for everyone. Not screenshot-checked yet.
- Built (review fixes, protocol 91): who is told what each tick is `ShooterCore/Jobs/JobFeed` (unit-tested), membership and Respect `CartelMembers`, the Contact's requests `CartelServer`. A refused join, quit or start answers the panel with event 38 `CartelRefused` (the refusal's code and words), shown in Start's (or Join's) reason line until the next build. Every reason (a failure, a refused start, join or quit, why no Respect) has one key and one text in `CartelWords` (ShooterCore), which the server writes and the client registers. The test kind starts only for an admin. Server text that names the interact key writes `{interact}` (`JobText`); the tracker shows the bound key.

## Simple jobs (v1)

- **Delivery**: its duffel lies on the **Cartel pallet** by the Contact (never in anyone's hands); carry it to a drop point before the timer runs out. The pallet has four bag spots, one per running Delivery; a fifth is refused ("The pallet is full."). A map without a pallet has no Delivery. The line and the marker are on the pallet ("Take the duffel from the pallet") until a party player carries it, then at the drop point. Loaded in a vehicle (**Cargo vehicles**) the line says to drive it to the drop point, take it out (the throw key) and hand it over, and the marker stays at the drop point; the hand-over is unchanged (carry it, hold the interact key within `radius_m`). The Delivery fails ("The objective was lost") when its duffel no longer exists for any reason but the Job's own end.
- **Hit**: kill a Rival dealer who stands outdoors at a random spot (zone kind `cartel_hit`) with a few guards (Gunmen and Enforcers). The guards attack the job's players on sight within a radius and ignore everyone else unless attacked. The target dying by anyone's hand is success.
- Built (#494, #497): `DeliveryJobKind` and `HitJobKind` (ShooterCore/Cartel, cartel.json `jobs.delivery`, `jobs.hit`), points claimed by `CartelSpots` (book key `cartel_drop:<i>` / `cartel_hit:<i>`), the party the Job's `JobParty` (the one set of a Job, refreshed by the `JobBook` each tick), the server's world `CartelJobWorld`. Delivery: a `product` Duffel lying on a free pallet spot (`CartelPallet`, book key `pallet:<i>`, freed when the Job ends; `DuffelBook.Place`; the spot is `pallet.spots` of cartel.json turned with the pallet); a party player picks it up (the interact key), then hold the interact key 2 s within 4 m of the drop point (the client's prompt "Hold <key>: hand the duffel over" and the hold ring, `CartelClient`, from `jobs.delivery` `radius_m` / `hold_seconds`). Hit: the dealer is the Rival crew's Gang boss (no new kind), guards on a ring round him; marker on the spot, then on him (`Hit.Target`). Test with `/cartel job delivery|hit [tier]`.

## Heists (v1)

### Template

1. The leader starts it at the Contact; the Cartel van appears on the road beside the Contact.
2. The squad drives the van to the objective (Harvest: the plantation itself). Until a party player is at the van (within 8 m) or sits in it (`Heist.Phase` `ToVan`), the Job's marker is on the van, then on the objective as before.
3. They fill duffels and throw them along a chain into the van's back: **loaded**.
4. Assaults come to the objective the moment the van first enters its area (no grace), then every interval.
5. The drop is a **Receiver** (see "Receivers"): the van with at least the minimum loaded drives to it, the party unloads the Duffels into it (carried or thrown, X takes them out of the van), and the heist succeeds the moment the Receiver is sent off with at least the minimum on it. Respect counts what is on the Receiver at that moment. Assaults stop once the van leaves the objective area on the way to the drop, where Rival **chase cars** take over (see "Chase cars"), and come again at the drop (the first the moment the van enters its area, then every interval, until the end).

### Duffels

- One per player. Carrying one: no sprint, slower by its weight (weed light, cash medium, product heavy); the carrier can still shoot.
- One press of the throw key (X) throws it at once, 8 m on flat ground (`duffels.json` `throw_m`), the same for every kind: the weight slows the carrier only. No charge, and no putting it down at the feet; it lands as a lying duffel (a world entity). Only the job's players pick them up.
- No vehicle seat while carrying (throw it in the van first). Death drops it where the carrier fell.
- Built: `DuffelBook.Throw` (the arc: `lift_deg`, the elevation limits, wall stop, flight cap; a throw into a cargo volume that takes it loads), the throw key a plain fresh press (`Edge` in `DuffelServer.OnInput` and `DuffelsClient`; the machine `Duffel.ThrowKey` and the client's charge ring are gone). `DuffelBook.Drop` remains only for what the player does not choose (death, a seat, leaving the party).
- Loaded in a vehicle (below), a duffel is drawn on its slot there on every client, fixed to the vehicle; no seat is taken by the carrier either way.
- Built (Delivery rework): `DuffelBook` has every live cargo vehicle as a cargo volume (`VehicleCargo`, ShooterCore/Cartel), a Duffel is `Loaded` with its `Volume`, `Slot` and `LoadOrder`; `DuffelBook.Takeable` / `TakeOut` take the last loaded one of the party out, `RemoveLoadedIn` destroys a lost vehicle's. Wire: a loaded duffel's owner field is the vehicle's world id and bits 4-7 of its state byte the slot (`DuffelRules.Pack` / `SlotOf`).

### Cargo vehicles

Which vehicles take Duffels, and how (vehicles.json `cargo_volume`; `VehicleCargoVolumeDef`, `CargoVolume`; protocol 93):

- Types: `truck` (8, an open bed), `car` (1, the boot), `golf_mk2` (2, the hatch), `cartel_van` (12, the bay). No other vehicle takes bags. A cargo area has an **access point** on the ground at its end (`access`, `access_radius_m`): where a player stands to work the doors and to take a bag out.
- **Doors** (`cargo_volume.doors`: the boot lid, the hatch, the van's rear doors; machine `Cargo.Doors`, `CargoDoors`): Closed or Open on the server. A player on foot within the radius of the access point presses the interact key (E) to open or shut them, anybody's vehicle; they stay as left, also while driving; a spawn, a reset, a respawn or a destruction shuts them. Everywhere else round the vehicle E still enters it. Every client swings the doors (`CargoDoorsView`) from event 45 `CargoDoors` (u16 vehicle, u8 open; a joiner is told each open one). The prompt is "<key>  Open the boot" / "Close the boot" (`label` per type: boot, hatch, rear doors). At an open boot with a bag of the party's in it both keys work, so both show in one line: "X  Take a duffel out  ·  E  Close the boot" (same for the rear doors and the hatch). The truck has no doors: always open, no door prompt.
- **Who has the key at the access point** (`KeyCompetitor.CargoDoors`, `InputServer`): a lying Duffel's pick-up first (a bag that fell beside the vehicle is picked up with the same key), then the Loot and the village doors, then the cargo doors, then the vehicle's seat (and a carrier's "no seat" notice). So at the access point E works the doors, not the seat; one step away it enters. The plantation's hold gives way to all of them.
- **Loading** (`VehicleCargo`, `CargoServer`): a thrown duffel is loaded when its flight meets the volume's box (grown by `duffels.json` `cargo_margin_m`, so a throw that lands against the open doors still counts) and the volume accepts it: doors open (or none), fewer than `capacity` loaded, the party allowed (any party's for an ordinary vehicle; the Cartel van only its own Job's, `Restrict`) and, for a `from_top` bed (the truck), the duffel came down through the top face. One that meets a cargo vehicle it does not accept stops as at a wall and falls straight down beside it (a truck's bed met from the side or the tail; a closed boot, a full one, another party's van); one that meets a shut lid's top slides off by the nearest side. It is never loaded.
- **Loaded duffels** take the first free slot (`slots`), are drawn there (`DuffelsClient.PlaceLoaded`, on the hull as it leans) with no physics and no animation, seen through the open doors and the windows. They are placed by `DuffelsClient.RenderLoaded`, called from `NetClient.RenderVehicles` right after the vehicles and the Receivers are drawn, from the transform the vehicle (or Receiver) has this frame: placed earlier in the frame they trailed the van by one frame of its motion (a bag outside the van at speed). A Receiver's bags go the same way.
- **Taking out**: a party player on foot with empty hands within the radius of the access point of a vehicle whose doors are open (or a truck) presses the throw key (X): the last loaded duffel of their party in it goes into their hands. Not their party's: nothing. The server tells the client when that would work (event 44 `DuffelTake`: vehicle, kind) for the prompt "<key>  Take a duffel out" and the predicted burden; the take-out prompt outranks the doors' prompt (both keys still work).
- A vehicle that is destroyed, retired, despawned or reset (a spawn, a respawn, a fall out of the world, a new round) loses its loaded duffels (removed from the world: `ServerCar.LifeEnded`). A Delivery whose duffel is gone fails "The objective was lost"; a Heist fails when its van is destroyed (`HeistFacts.VanDestroyed`), as before.
- A Heist counts only its own Cartel van's bay (`VehicleCargo.Loaded`, the one count: a duffel taken out lowers it) and tells the party at the minimum and the maximum; there is no second count.
- Not screenshot- or play-checked: the doors' look, the slots' fit, the access radius and the prompts want a human look (docs/standards/car-design.md "Hand back for a human look").

### Cartel van

- A new vehicle `cartel_van`, the Gang Warfare `SM_Veh_Van_01` imported by flow 1 of `docs/standards/car-design.md` (drive model, ADR 0036): driver and passenger seats behind glass, a rear cargo volume that takes duffels.
- Never dealt (`spawn_count` 0); only a heist puts one down, and it goes when the job ends.
- Only the job's players can take a seat in it, and only the job's own party's duffels load into its bay (its rear doors work like any cargo vehicle's: the key at the access point, shut at first; **Cargo vehicles**); everyone else is refused a seat.
- High hit points, destructible; destroyed, the heist fails.
- **Loaded speed limit**: with any duffel loaded the van goes at most `loaded_max_kmh` (cartel.json, next to `van_type` of each Heist; 60 km/h); empty it drives as vehicles.json says. It is a governor on the driver's throttle (`SpeedGovernor`, ShooterCore/Vehicles, a pure function; `DriveModel` is untouched): the forward key eases off over the last 2 m/s below the cap and is nothing at it, so the van coasts; brake, reverse, steering and the handbrake are as always, and a van already faster (a bag thrown in while it drove) is not braked, it only gets no more push. The server applies it in `ServerCar.StepDrive` (`ServerCar.SpeedCap`, set by `CargoServer` from `VehicleCargo.Loaded`) and the driver's predicting client in `DrivePrediction.SpeedCapMps` (set by `DriveClient` from the Duffels it already replicates, `DuffelsClient.SpeedCapOf`): same input, same speed, no wire change, no rubber-banding but the one moment a bag is loaded or taken out (the client learns it a round trip after the server). `CartelFile.LoadedCapMps(vehicleType)` is the one reading of the cap.

### Assaults

Reworked (#437, after the playtest of 2026-11-28): an Assault is no longer the Thieves' scripted Raid van but a real vehicle driven like a chase car. Numbers: `cartel.json` top-level `assault_car` (`ShooterCore/Cartel/AssaultCar.cs`: `AssaultCarData`, `AssaultSpawn`, `AssaultStall`, `AssaultRules`) and, per heist, `assault_*`, `max_*`; server `Net/Server/Cartel/AssaultFleet.cs` on the shared `ChaseUnit` / `ChaseServer`.

- **When** (machine `Heist.Assaults`: Idle, Waiting, Paused, DropWaiting, Over; `AssaultClock`): **no grace**. The first Assault is due the moment the Cartel van first enters the objective's area, then one every `assault_interval_seconds` (40, 36, 32, 28, 24 by tier: 40% of the old 100 to 60) while it is there; both heists. On the road to the drop it pauses (Assaults do not come on the drive; chase cars do), and the time left is kept. At the drop the first is due the moment the van first enters the Receiver's area, then every interval, to the heist's end. No new Assault while `max_assaults_alive` (3) of the heist's are in the fight; the clock goes on.
- **The vehicle**: the `cartel_van` in its own dark skin (skin 1; the player's Cartel van keeps skin 0, white). Skin chosen as the chase car's red was, by mean luminance: of the Gang Warfare atlases Vehicle_01 to 06, measured over the van body's own UV texels (area-weighted, 0 to 255): 92.9, 93.6, 91.6, 95.0, **48.7 (05)**, 48.8 (06); `scripts/vehicles/specs/cartel_van.json` skins, generated by `standardize_vehicle.py` (the GLBs are in the git-ignored private pack). Destroyable like any car: a destroyed one kills the riders still in it (as a chase car's); its wreck lies `wreck_seconds` then goes.
- **The crew** (5: 1 melee Enforcer at the wheel and 4 Gunmen; `assault_enforcers` / `assault_gunmen` per tier [4,4,4,4,5], `gunmen_per_extra_member` 1 more Gunman per extra member, `max_crew` 6; the Enforcers are kept first, so a bigger squad adds Gunmen and never a second melee member; `AssaultRules.SeatOrder`): the cab has 2 seats, so the driver (seat 0) is the Enforcer, the first Gunman sits in the passenger seat and shoots from the window like a chase car's Gunmen, and the others ride seated on the **bay posts** (`assault_car.bay_posts`: four vehicle-frame points on the bay floor, x -0.5 / +0.5, z 0.45 and 1.35, y 0.73, measured from the bay: the spec's floor y 0.73, bulkhead source z 0.271, rear doors' hinge source z -2.295; the game frame is z = 0.074 - source z, x mirrored: the bay is z -0.197 to 2.369). A bay rider is a Gang member posted with `GangPost.Shut` (no window): it never fights while it rides, and is drawn seated (the seated bit). When the van stops they step out **behind it** (the rear doors: the post's x, `bay_exit_z_m` 3.6 along the van's frame, 1.07 m behind its tail, snapped to the navigation mesh), and walk back to the same point to get in again (the remount rule). The van's bay takes nobody's Duffels (sealed).
- **Where** (`AssaultSpawn`, replaces the map edge and the Raid planner): a road point (one every 30 m) or open drivable ground point (rings round the van) whose **drivable way to the Cartel van** (the length of the way it would drive: `OffRoadRoute`, roads where they bring it closer, open ground where that is shorter) is **250 to 400 m**, at least 150 m from every player, 25 m from the other Rival vehicles (`spawn_apart_m`), no vehicle beside it (6 m: the chase's own check). **No out-of-sight rule**: the drive in is the grace. **Nearest first**: candidates under 0.6 x the band's minimum of straight distance are dropped, the others are looked at by straight distance to the van ascending from the band's minimum up (the ones between 0.6 x and 1 x of it after those), shuffled only within 50 m buckets, so a spot on a nearby track (3 m tracks are in the graph) wins over a village a kilometre off; at most 12 ways worked out (`spawn_checks`, spent in that order: **one budget for all the bands together**, each is a planned way: it costs a few ms; a band that uses it up leaves none to the wider ones). **When none fits the band it widens rather than send none**: 200 to 600 m, then 100 to 600 m (`spawn_bands_m`: no band's max may be past `give_up_m`, checked, or the van would give up at once); the server logs the band used, and why when none fits at all (no candidate point, or no way within the widest band).
- **How it drives and fights**: exactly a chase car (machine `Chase.Car`, `ChaseUnit`; the heist's own `chase` driving numbers): the target is the Cartel van itself, over roads and open ground, re-planned every `repath_seconds`, at most 84 km/h (`max_speed_mps`), 120 km/h while it is more than `far_m` (100) behind the van (`far_speed_mps` 33; the Cartel van type's top speed is 33, so it just reaches it); a van coming at another chase car or Assault head-on is met as a chase car meets it (Waiting, see "Chase cars"); the Gunman at the window shoots within `shoot_range_m` of the van; it stops and the crew gets out only when the Cartel van is slower than `stop_speed_mps` (5) within `stop_within_m` (30) (or its driver is dead, or it is stuck near the van), and gets back in when the van pulls away (`remount_m`, `pull_mps`). A van with its crew out fights on foot as a Rival crew against the Job's party on sight. **Gives up**: `assault_car.give_up_m` **600** for 20 s (the chase car's 250 would fire at once from a spawn 250 to 400 m away). **Crew on foot**: it posts at the objective's centre (`Goal.Centre`, snapped onto the navigation mesh, as the old Assault did) while the Heist is at the objective (`Heist.Phase` AtObjective), else at the Cartel van (on the road, at the drop); a chase car's crew keeps posting at the van (`RivalProfile.Post`).
- **In the fight** (`max_assaults_alive`): while its crew lives or rides; a standing vehicle with a dead crew does not hold the cap; once the bodies are gone the vehicle goes (`ChaseCleanup`, wreck first). A stalled Assault does not count either.
- **Stalled** (machine `Assault.Stall`: Idle, Watching, Stalled; `AssaultStall`, now the one progress watch of every Rival vehicle, chase cars too, with the chase section's `stall_*` numbers): a vehicle whose crew rides (Chasing or Lost) and that is farther than `out_within_m` (60) from the van is watched; no 10 m (`stall_progress_m`) closer to the Cartel van in 10 s (`stall_seconds`) is stalled (within `stall_near_m` 40 of the van counts as close enough). ### Chase cars

Decided with the owner on 2026-11-27 (cartel-notes "Harvest rework"); built after the Receivers; reworked after the playtest of 2026-11-27 (off the roads, speed, the crew walking back, crews of 4). Numbers in `cartel.json` `jobs.<heist>.chase` (both heists) and `off_road`, the rules in `ShooterCore/Cartel/ChaseData.cs`, `ChaseCar.cs`, `ChaseRoad.cs`, `OffRoad.cs`; server `Net/Server/Cartel/ChaseServer.cs` (`ChaseServer`, `ChaseFleet`), `ChaseUnit.cs` and `OpenGround.cs`.

- **What**: a Rival car after the loaded van. The vehicles.json `car` type in its darkest skin (skin 3, the red one: the atlas swap of `skins`, no new skin; by the mean luminance of the catalogue renders red is 60, blue 85, green 86, yellow 131, grey 137), a Rival driver (an Enforcer, seat 0: the car's one melee member) and **3 Gunmen** (the car's other seats): 4 in all (`gunmen` [3,...], `max_gunmen` 3: the seats are full, so tier and squad size add nothing here). Killing them pays nothing (Rival crew rules).
- **When**: from the van leaving the objective with the minimum loaded (`HeistRun.LeftObjective`; a Trap house: leaving the house) the first after `first_after_seconds` (0: the moment it leaves), then one every `interval_seconds` (22), at most `max_alive` (3) in the fight at once (a car is in the fight while its crew sits in it or walks back to it, or a member lives; a due car held back by the cap comes the moment one is out). None new once the van reaches the drop (`ReachedDrop`); the ones out then fight on and join the drop's fight on foot. Machine `Heist.Chases` (`ChaseClock`: Idle, Running, Over; a Countdown inside); a due car that finds no spot asks again after `spawn_retry_seconds`.
- **Where** (reworked #437, 2026-11-28): **ahead** of the van first: the server plans the van's way to the drop's Receiver the way a chase plans a way (`OffRoadRoute`: roads where they bring it closer, open ground where that is shorter) and takes the points `spawn_ahead_min_m` to `spawn_ahead_max_m` (150 to 400) along it, as an **ambush**: the spot is not on the way but `ambush_side_m` (4.5: past a road's edge for a car) to either side of it (both tried), on open drivable ground (`OpenGround.Spot`; never the road's centre, otherwise dropped), facing **along** the van's way there (towards the drop, not towards the van), so it needs no U-turn. **No sight rule** for ahead spots: only no vehicle beside it (`ChaseServer.VehicleNear`), 150 m from every player, `spawn_apart_m` from the other chase cars and a way to the van (the party may see it stand). It starts in **Waiting** (see below). When nothing ahead fits (the drop is near, or no open ground beside the way) the old rule, with its sight rule: on open drivable ground behind the van along its way: the server keeps the van's trail since the objective (`ChaseTrail`, a point every 15 m, the objective's centre first) and takes the points `spawn_back_min_m` to `spawn_back_max_m` (150 to 500) behind it. A candidate is the nearest road within `spawn_road_m` of such a point, or open ground (`OpenGround.Spot`: ground, no water, nothing solid within the car's reach) at the point itself or `spawn_spread_m` (40) and half of it to either side of it, across the van's way: a field or a meadow, not only a road. It is at least `spawn_player_min_m` (150) from every player, out of the party's sight (a line-of-sight check from every human party member's eye, the same line a Gunman's sight uses; the party's own vehicle does not block it), `spawn_apart_m` from the other chase cars, clear of vehicles and has a way from it to the van (`OffRoadRoute`); one candidate at random (the next when it has no way), facing along the road, or straight, towards the van (`ChaseSpawn`). None fit yet (the van has not gone far enough, everyone is in sight): it waits and asks again.
- **How it drives**: it is a real vehicle, a `ServerCar` on the drive model, driven by an NPC's inputs on the server (`ServerCar.NpcInput`, stepped by the vehicle loop like a player's inputs). The way (`OffRoadRoute.Plan`, planned again every `repath_seconds` (1.5)) is the cheapest of: straight to the van over open ground, or onto the nearest road and along it, leaving it where the field to the van is shorter; a metre of open ground costs `off_road.open_cost` (1.3), a metre of road 1, and the line over open ground must be open (no building, trunk, fence, water or steep slope: `OpenGround.Line`). It is followed by a pure pursuit (`ChasePilot`: the harvester's AI steering with the drive model's own turn radius at the speed, `DriveModel.TurnRadiusAt`; the shared maths is untouched) that does not stop at the path's end (it steers on along the last leg towards the van, which moved on; the path is planned again each second and a half), and when no way is found the old one is kept a few seconds. It keeps `follow_m` from the van: the wanted speed is the van's plus `catch_up_gain` per metre of gap beyond it (less per metre short of it), at most `max_speed_mps`, or `far_speed_mps` while the gap is more than `far_m`, eased for a bend ahead, so it slows rather than close on the van and never aims at its body. **K-turn** (`ChasePilot.BackingRound`, a pure decision from the heading error and the signed speed, no state): when the way is behind the car (more than 110 degrees off its nose) and it is slower than 4 m/s it backs up while steering (the nose turns to the way) until the way is under 60 degrees off, then drives on (a turning circle of about 17 m at 7 m/s wedges between houses).
- **Speed** (rubber band): `max_speed_mps` is 23.33 (84 km/h), 1.4 times the loaded Cartel van's 60 km/h cap, while the gap to the van is `far_m` (100) or less; further behind it is `far_speed_mps` 33 (120 km/h; `ChaseRules.FollowSpeed`, the chase car and the Assault van both). An NPC's input only: the `car` type's own top speed is 44.4 m/s (160 km/h) and the `cartel_van`'s 33, so both reach it, no allowance on the drive model is needed and the car a player drives is unchanged.
- **Waiting** (machine `Chase.Car` state `Waiting`; new keys `ambush_side_m` 4.5, `wait_max_seconds` 40, `meet_m` 70): (a) a car put down ahead starts in Waiting (Spawned to Waiting): it stands (handbrake; `ChasePilot.Wait`), its Gunmen shoot from the windows within `shoot_range_m` (a standing aim), and it goes to Chasing once the van has passed it (the car lies behind the van along the van's heading: `ChaseRules.Passed`; for the ambush the van's point is then ahead of the car's nose), after `wait_max_seconds`, or when the van stays more than `give_up_m` away for `give_up_seconds`. The stop rule (van slower than `stop_speed_mps` within `stop_within_m`, or its driver dead) also applies from Waiting (Stopping, the crew out). (b) A car already Chasing that the van comes at head-on (the van faster than `stop_speed_mps`, within `meet_m`, the two headings more than 120 degrees apart, the van ahead of its nose: `ChaseRules.HeadOn`; e.g. an Assault van driving in along the road the Cartel van leaves by) pulls aside (brake, the wheel to the right while it still moves) and waits the same way, then Chasing once the van has passed (the K-turn turns it round).
- **Wedged**: a car under 1 m/s with the van beyond `follow_m` for `unstick_after_seconds` (2.5) backs out (Reversing, `reverse_seconds` 1.5; `ChasePilot.Reverse`: the key against its heading, steered to turn its nose towards its way) and drives on again, up to `max_unsticks` (3) times in a row (forgotten after 3 s at 3 m/s); then the stuck clock (`stalled_seconds`) stops it as before, and a car with no way at all stops likewise.
- **How it fights**: within `shoot_range_m` (40) of the van the Gunmen shoot from the windows (their rounds hurt players and vehicles as any Gunman's: a round that stops on the van's hull damages it; the car's own hull is not in the way of their sight and rounds, nor is the hull of any other car that carries a chase crew (a rear car's riders do not shoot up the front car: `GangRound`), and the hull of the vehicle a player rides in no longer hides them from a Gunman's sight (`GangServer.Clear`), so the party in the van is fought: its round stops on the van's hull), with every round turned off its line by up to `moving_aim_error_deg` while the car moves faster than `moving_mps` (the gun's own aim error while it stands). Farther than that the crew holds fire. When the van stops or is under `stop_speed_mps` (5) within `stop_within_m` (30) of the car, the car stops and the crew gets out through the door on its seat's side and fights on foot as a Rival crew (the Job's party on sight, as an Assault's crew; they head for the van's place). A van off the road is followed over open ground, not given up on at the road's end.
- **Remount**: a crew on foot whose van is more than `remount_m` (60) from its nearest living member and pulling away (the gap grows faster than `pull_mps`, smoothed, for `remount_confirm_seconds`) walks back to its car (`GangMemberState.Remount`: a route over the navigation mesh to its seat's door, never fighting on the way), sits down in the seats it had when it spawned and the chase goes on. Only when the car is not wrecked, no player sits in it and its driver lives; a wrecked or taken car: the crew stays on foot (as a car with a dead driver). `remount_seconds` (30) to do it, then the crew stays on foot and tries again after `remount_retry_seconds` (10). The seats are reserved for nobody again while the crew walks back.
- **Losing them**: a car, or its crew on foot or walking back, more than `give_up_m` (250) from the van for `give_up_seconds` (20) gives up and is removed with its crew. A dead driver: the car rolls to a stop (no input) and the Gunmen get out (a dead passenger changes nothing). It takes damage and is destroyed like any car (bullets, bumps, crashes, blasts); a destroyed one kills the crew still inside, as it does the riders of any vehicle, and the wreck lies `wreck_seconds` (20, under the type's destroyed_return_time) then goes. The Job's end removes every car and crew.
- **Machine** `Chase.Car` (`ChaseCar`; the Assault vans run on it too, see "Assaults": one unit type, `ChaseUnit`, with a `RivalProfile` of vehicle, crew, spawn rule and give-up distance): Spawned, Chasing, Waiting, Reversing, Stopping, Out, Remounting, GivingUp, Gone, Lost (`Waiting`, then `Lost`, appended to the enum last; `ChaseState` is not on the wire). - **Driving through bends and narrow ways** (`ChasePilot.CurveSpeed`, `Follow`): the speed the car wants is capped by the bends ahead: along the path up to its braking distance (v squared over twice `brake`) plus the look-ahead, at every 5 m the radius is read from three points 5 m apart, the speed allowed there is sqrt(`corner_grip` 0.7 x the car type's `max_lateral_accel` x radius), and the car may be as fast as sqrt(that squared + 2 x 0.8 x `brake` x the distance to it), the least over the points: it brakes before a bend (the heading-error cut for a target far off the nose stays). The look-ahead is clamp(0.35 x speed + 4, 5, 14) m (less corner cutting). On a road edge narrower than `off_road.track_width_m` (4: tracks 3.0, service 3.5) it keeps to `track_speed_mps` (13), on open ground (no road edge within 4 m) to `open_speed_mps` (12); `max_speed_mps` / `far_speed_mps` hold on wide roads. **Route cost**: a metre of an edge narrower than `track_width_m` costs `off_road.track_cost` (1.5) in `OffRoadRoute` (`RoadGraph.Dijkstra` takes an optional per-width cost; the Thieves' Raid planner passes none: unchanged), so wide roads win when they are not much longer.

- **Not a vehicle** of `vehicles.json`: a Receiver is its own server thing (`ShooterCore/Cartel/Receiver.cs`: a world entity of type 59 and a cargo volume in the Duffel book). A vehicle would bring seats, a driver, damage, a body to push and a record in the vehicle snapshot, none of which it has; the cargo volume reuses `VehicleCargoVolumeDef`, `VehicleCargo` and the Duffels' loading rules whole, so a thrown Duffel that reaches its volume loads (carried, thrown or dropped onto it), a Receiver takes only its own Job's party's and never gives one back (no take-out). Its pilot, boatman and buyer are Cartel men (a Gang Warfare character drawn as the Contact is, `ContactView.Figure`) with no body in the world: nothing can hurt them. Bags on a Receiver are safe.
- **Slots**: as many as the heist's maximum, at most 15 (a Duffel's slot rides in four bits): stacked, the first layer first. Measured from the models, not guessed (the numbers and what they come from are in the comments of `cartel.json`): the pallet 8 + 7 on its deck (0.235), the helicopter 12 + 3 on the free floor of the cabin's left half (floor 0.732), the boat 6 + 6 + 3 on the cockpit floor between the bench and the console (floor 0.21).
- **Unloading** at the drop: the party takes the bags out of the Cartel van (the throw key at the open rear doors, as before) and carries or throws them into the Receiver. A bag on the Receiver rides with it: the entity's pose moves, the client draws each Duffel from the Receiver's pose and its slot.
- **The end**: a helicopter or boat leaves by itself once every one of the Job's Duffels is on board (at least the minimum), or early when the **leader** presses the interact key at the pilot or boatman with at least the minimum on board (the prompt "<key>  Tell the pilot to leave"; fewer on board: the reason "Not enough on board: n of at least m duffels"; a squad mate: that only the leader decides). A stash house: the leader presses the key at the buyer ("Hand over to the buyer") with at least the minimum on the pallet. Success is the moment it leaves (or the buyer takes it); Respect counts the Duffels on it then (the tier's Respect plus `bonus_respect_per_duffel` times the tier per Duffel above the minimum). The key is the ordinary interact press the server already reads (`ReceiverServer.Use` in `InputServer`; `KeyCompetitor.Receiver` keeps the plantation's hold off it); event 51 `ReceiverReach` tells the party what the press would do, for the prompt.
- **Leaving** is scripted, no physics, and every client sees it (the entity's position, yaw and pitch move each tick, interpolated): the helicopter climbs `climb_m` (60) turning to face the way it flies, then flies off over the nearest map edge nose down and is removed past it; the boat sails its placed route (`sail_mps`) to the edge and is removed at its last point. The Job ends at the success: everything of it goes then, but the sent-off Receiver and the Duffels on it finish their way and go with it (`ReceiverServer.Release`). A Job that fails while the Receiver still receives takes it away at once.
- **Assaults at the drop**: once the van is within the Receiver's `area_m` (the phase AtDrop, machine `Heist.Phase`) the Assault clock runs again (`Heist.Assaults`: DropWaiting): the first the moment the van enters the area, then every `assault_interval_seconds`, until the heist ends (`max_assaults_alive` holds); the Assault van is put down 250 to 400 m of way from the Cartel van and drives at it. Leaving the area pauses it, coming back goes on where it was.
- **Seam of the chase cars** (built, see "Chase cars"): `HeistRun.LeftObjective` ("the van left the objective with the minimum", the phase change AtObjective to ToDrop) and `HeistRun.ReachedDrop` ("the van reached the drop", to AtDrop), from `Heist.PhaseChanged`; `HeistRun.Receiver`, `VanFeet` and `Heist` say where everything is.
- **Objective lines and marker** (`Heist.Line`, `Heist.Marker`, `Heist.Drop` = `DropText`): "Go to the Cartel van, then drive it to the plantation" (the marker on the van), then "Drive the Cartel van to the plantation" (the marker on the objective); at the objective the marker is on the van while loading; once the van holds the minimum and is still in the objective's area the line is "Leave the area: n loaded (up to max)" and nothing is marked; once the van is out, "Drive the van to the helicopter: n loaded" (the marker on the drop), "Unload the duffels into the helicopter: n/min", "n on board (up to max): unload the rest, then tell the pilot to leave", "Delivered: n duffels". The party's chat gets the milestones too, each once per Heist (a bag taken out and thrown back tells nothing again).
- **Map data**: zone kinds `cartel_heli` (a drawn pad: the helicopter stands on its mean point, the nose toward the middle of its first edge) and `cartel_boat` (a drawn line: the first point is the mooring, the bow toward the second, the rest the route to the map's edge, in the order drawn; the outline's closing edge is no part of it, so the route must not cross the chord back to the mooring). Both are Runtime zones exported as outlines (`zone_polygons.json`, like the plantation), no points and no density rule; read as `CartelMap.Helis` / `Boats` (`ReceiverSpot`) and claimed with `CartelSpots` (`cartel_heli:<i>`, `cartel_boat:<i>`). Placed in `pipeline/config/edits.json`: three landing spots in open flat fields (Z-0068 to Z-0070) and two moorings on the east side of the west river (Z-0071, Z-0072: at the bank by the road, depth 1.8 to 2 m, Z-0071's route turns west off the map at about y 505; Z-0072's follows the river north-east to about y 700, 2.2 m or deeper all the way). A map with none of a kind has none of it.
