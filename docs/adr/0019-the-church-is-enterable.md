# The church is enterable

Extends ADR 0009 (bunkers are the only buildings with an interior): for the Holy Mass event (`docs/events/README.md`) the village church becomes the second, and so far last, building with an interior. Everything else stays a solid block.

**1. Why the church.** The event needs a nave, an altar, pews, a gallery and a place for a demon to crawl in; a citizen walks in through a door and sits. A solid church cannot host that, and it is the one building worth the work: one landmark, one footprint, one event.

**2. The code model stays.** The church is still `church_solids` in `landmarks.py`, now with `church_interior.py` building the hollow shell (walls with a 2.0 m doorway, floor slab, gallery, nave vault, chancel) instead of the solid nave and apse blocks. It fits the real footprint, so no hand model (map #220, #221) is waited for. Rejected: a hand-made church asset (blocked on #221 and cannot follow the footprint).

**3. Real-shell collision, as for bunkers.** The collision mesh is the walls with their opening, the floors, the gallery and the stair ramp; the loader needs no change. The floor is a real slab at porch height (0.25 m), because the ground under a building is flattened and a player must not step in the doorway. The stairs to the gallery are a collision ramp (the visual has steps): no ladder state, no new movement code, bots and citizens walk a scripted path up them.

**4. `church.json` is the contract between the pipeline and the game.** It carries `doorway` (middle of the opening on the floor, outward compass yaw, width, height), `rooms` (nave and chancel as volumes, grown into the walls), seats with their routes, standing spots, pastor and monk spots, demon anchors and furniture poses. Points are `[x east, y north, z up]` like the other map JSON. A map built before it has no `church.json` or no `doorway`: the game then has no church doors, no church shelter and no Mass, and nothing else changes.

**5. Furniture is game-placed private Synty props.** Pews, altar, lectern and holy water are placed by the game at the `props` poses of `church.json` (`ChurchProps`), as Synty models from the gitignored `assets-private/`. The pack is paid: it never enters the public repo or the exported map, and there is no fallback model; without the pack the church is empty. The collision boxes of the props are made on the server too.

**6. The doors are closed outside the event.** A two-state door (closed or open) in the doorway: a box on the World layer, so players, bullets and citizens stop at it, on server and clients; drawn as dark wooden double doors, open = leaves swung inward. Default closed, so the interior is not walkable outside the Mass; the event opens and closes them (and the demon fight locks players in). The server owns the state and replicates it as one reliable event (`ChurchDoor`, 1 byte; a joiner is told when they are open). Closing never shuts through a body: whoever stands in the doorway is moved to the nearer side, everyone else stays where they are (they may be locked in). While the doors are closed and the camera is outside, the client does not draw the furniture (the shell is baked into the map mesh and is not split).

**7. The church is a shelter.** The `rooms` act like bunker rooms for splash damage (ADR 0009 §5): the church rooms are loaded into the same `Shelters`, with the same rules and exceptions (the MOAB and a direct hit do not ask). Nave and chancel are one room. An open door does not change this: it is the same as the open bunker doorway, a blast fired in through it hurts nobody outside. Keeping the rule independent of the door state keeps it one rule and one table, and the cost (a rocket through the open door is harmless to the outside) is small for a closed-friends game.
