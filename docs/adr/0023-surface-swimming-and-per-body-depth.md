# Surface-only swimming, per-body depth grids and carved beds

Supersedes the swim, submerge and breath parts of `docs/adr/0004` and its flat bed (one depth per body). Decided for the "better water" design (issue #229, with #231 for the real depth of the Morava). Builds on ADR 0002 (no new wire fields) and ADR 0004 (the water file and its per-parcel bodies, which stay).

**1. The player swims at the surface only.** In deep water (column depth at least `WaterConsts.SwimDepth`, 1.1 m) the body floats: the feet hang `SwimFeetBelowSurface` (1.25 m, the standing eye height less the eyes' 0.4 m above the surface) under the surface, and there is no vertical swim input. Crouch does not dive; a jump does nothing, except that a jump within `ShoreHopDistance` (1.5 m) of the bank hops out onto it. Sprint is off in deep water. Swim speed stays 3 m/s. Shooting works as on land: water does not obstruct bullets.

**Why.** Diving needed a breath meter, drowning damage, an air HUD, a weapon lock under the eyes, a full-screen underwater view and an audio low-pass, and none of it added play once the player can swim anywhere. A surface swim keeps the one penalty (speed) and the splash on entry. The floating body is decided by the shared movement step from `WaterDepth`, `WaterSurfaceY` and `ShoreDistance` filled by `Water.Sample`, so prediction and the server agree with no new wire field.

**2. Wading is continuous.** The speed is the land speed at ankle depth (0.2 m), eases to 0.6 of it at 0.9 m, and blends into the swim speed at 1.1 m (`Wading.Speed`). A landing in water keeps part of the fall damage by depth (`Wading.LandingDamageShare`): all of it dry or at the ankles, half at 0.9 m, none from 1.1 m. The helicopter's water damage is unchanged.

**3. Removed.** Crouch-to-dive and the vertical swim speed and acceleration; `Breath` (capacity, refill and drowning damage) and `MoveState.HeadUnder` and `AirSpent`; the AIR HUD; the weapon lock under water; the full-screen underwater view (`UnderwaterView`, `underwater.gdshader`) and the SFX low-pass. The sniper's breath hold is a different thing and stays.

**4. Depth comes from a grid, and the terrain is carved to it.** A water body's bed is no longer flat. The pipeline generates a natural bed from each outline (depth grows with the distance from the bank, with noise for pools and riffles, and the outer side of bends deeper), and lowers the 4 m terrain grid under the water to it, so walking, wading, the visible bed and the depth query agree. The file is `water.json` version 2: a per-body depth grid and flow samples. `Water.Parse` still accepts version 1 (a flat bed). This is the pipeline's slice of the design (its own change); the shooter only asks `Water.Depth`, `DepthAt`, `Sample`, `ShoreDistance` and `BodyAt`, and does not depend on how the grid is stored.

**5. Flow and waves are later slices.** A current on the Morava (a centreline of the polygon chain, pushing swimmers and boats but not waders) is planned for the flow slice. The wave function is cosmetic, drives the shader and the boats' visual bob, and does not move the swim height or the hit detection.

**Consequences.** Riffles about 0.8 m deep are wadeable, so the river has natural crossings. Players cannot walk along a river floor at surface level, because the terrain is carved. Boat physics and hit detection keep the flat surface. A player on the 1.1 to 1.25 m band rests on the bed while floating, since the feet would otherwise sink into it; the eyes are then a little lower than 0.4 m (flagged for playtest). Maps built before version 2 keep a flat bed and the same shooter rules.
