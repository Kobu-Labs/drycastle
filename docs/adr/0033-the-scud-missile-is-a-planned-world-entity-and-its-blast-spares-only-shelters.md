# The Scud missile is a planned world entity, and its blast is spared only by blast shelters

Decided for the Scud truck (GLOSSARY "Scud truck", "Launcher", "Scud missile", "Scud blast"). Builds on ADR 0031 (plan the flight at the fire), ADR 0025 (world interest), ADR 0009 and 0019 (shelters).

**The launcher is a server machine that the crew drives with commands.** `Scud.Launcher` (Stowed, Targeting, Proposed, Erecting, Launching, Spent, Lowering, Empty, Reloading, Aborting) lives on the server per truck. The client only draws it (the rack angle is a pure function of state and elapsed time, `ScudRules.RackAngle`, so the wire carries the state, its clock and the angle an abort lowers from, never an angle per tick). The lock is a *proposal*: every occupied seat, the operator's included, must confirm it, so a click cannot launch by itself and a solo player still needs two deliberate acts. Anything that changes the crew or moves the truck drops the proposal; the rack keeps rising once confirmed even if the crew leaves, so a launch cannot be cancelled by killing the driver after the point is locked (only the operator may abort, before the ignition ends).

**The missile is not simulated and cannot be hit.** At the ignition's end the server plans the whole flight (`ScudFlightPlan`: a vertical boost, then one Bezier arc whose first speed is the boost's last and whose last direction is the dive) and the entity only walks along it. It is a world entity of its own type (11), not an Aircraft and not a rocket: no hit points, no Lock, no sweep, so the terrain it passes over does not matter and a hill between the truck and the target does not stop it. Every client interpolates it like any entity; the siren and the map marker come from the launch event (which carries the target and the flight time), so nothing needs the entity to be in range.

**The blast ignores walls inside its lethal radius and respects shelters everywhere.** `ScudBlastServer.Blast` is the single hook (the missile's impact calls it; a shot rocket cart will). Inside 60 m a player dies in a vehicle or out of it, spawn protected or not, unless a Bunker room or the church (`MapAreas.Shelters.Reaches`) is between them and the blast; a house is not. Between 60 m and 150 m it is an ordinary explosion (damage falling off, walls and a covered seat's hull take their share through `SplashShare`). Vehicles in 60 m are doomed, farther ones lose hit points; glass to 250 m shatters outright (`BlastGlass` with a centre damage above any pane's hit points). This is the opposite choice from the MOAB, which kills everyone and asks no shelter, and from a rocket, which asks every wall.

**The remote view is the whole loaded map.** The operator's camera is `Player.Turret` straight down from a fixed height; the terrain, buildings, vehicles and players are on every client at every range (the players and vehicles channel has no interest filter), so they are in the picture. Animals, flocks and droppings are filtered to 800 m of the player's own body (ADR 0025) and so appear in the feed only around the truck. The feed is the operator's camera relayed to the rest of the crew at five a second, and it is smoothed on the client.

**Consequences.** One rocket per truck and one truck for the match (`returns: false`): the pressure is on the event, the rocket cart and the shelters, not on a respawn timer. The truck's rack is not a hit volume while it is raised. The client's own screen mode is a machine too (`Scud.Screen`), because the screen, the pending server answer and the cancels were a flag cluster in the making. Protocol 68.
