# The intel room is the command post of one bunker

Decided for issue #262 (event announcements), building on ADR 0009. An announced event ("suspicious activity detected, report to the base") needs a place to report to, with a big screen that says what is coming. ADR 0009 allows exactly one kind of interior, the bunker, so the place is a bunker. This does not change ADR 0009; it uses the command post it already furnishes.

> **Amended 2026-11-13: the intel room moved to the military hangar.** Points 1 and 2 and the "three ladders" consequence below no longer hold on the village map; the rest (3 to 5, the alternatives) stands. The base is now the first placed object of an asset named in `intel.json` (`base_asset`: `pack:synty-military/SM_Bld_Hangar_01`, today `o-0582`), the room is its floor, and the screen hangs on the inside of the far end wall facing the entrance. Why: the hangar is one big room at ground level, no ladders, and a place that looks like a base; the bunker stays an ordinary building.
> - The screen's place is data, not code: `intel.json` `room` gives it in the object's own frame (floor height, `at` [x, z], `facing`, lower edge, size). The anchor is computed from the object's position, turn and scale in objects.json (`IntelRoom.Place`), never from the model, so a player without the paid pack (a placeholder box) gets the screen at the same spot, floating where the hangar would be.
> - The prefab is closed: its doors are solid and both ends have them. `room.open_parts` names prefab nodes to hide and stop colliding (the small door of the entrance end); the screen end stays shut.
> - The bunker anchor stays as a fallback only: a map without a `base_asset` object (an older export) uses the command post of `base_bunker` and the wall the pipeline still reserves (`bunkers.json` `intel`, point 2). With neither, no intel room.
> - The map must be exported again for the hangar to be in `objects.json`.

**1. The intel room is the lowest floor (the command post) of one bunker, the base.** The three bunkers each have a ground floor and three rooms under it, the lowest furnished as a command post (table, generator, radio). That is the room the issue describes, it already exists and no new building or interior is needed. `godot/shooter/data/intel.json` names the bunker (`base_bunker`, an OSM way id from `bunkers.json`). It is `w378900206`, the one nearest the church and the main square (about 230 m; the other two are 300 m and 590 m away); changing the file moves the room.

**2. The pipeline reserves the wall.** The floors under the ground have no openings in their walls (the ground floor has a doorway and up to four firing slits, one at the middle of each wall, which leaves only short stretches), but they do have furniture the pipeline places by fitting it along the longest walls. A screen placed by the game alone would sit behind a locker in some bunkers (without the reserved strip, two of the three real command posts have furniture in front of the screen's wall). So `bunker.py` picks the widest clear stretch of straight wall of the command post (1.6 to 3.0 m wide, 1.1 m high, 0.85 m above the floor), keeps the furniture out of the 0.9 m in front of it, and exports it as `bunkers[].intel` in `bunkers.json` (position, facing, heights, width). Nothing else of the mesh changes. A bunker without such a wall has no `intel`, and the game then has no intel room (the announcement still works). Checked on the three real footprints: all have one (3.0, 3.0 and 2.7 m).

**3. The screen is a client-only texture.** The game draws a frame and a quad at that anchor; the quad shows a `SubViewport` with the screen's UI (`IntelScreenView`), laid out in the texture's own pixels (320 px per metre of wall) with the shared theme's five text sizes. It is not an object, has no collision and cannot be shot. It renders only within 14 m of the camera (`view_range_m`) and then about once a second, so it costs nothing elsewhere. With several events it shows one at a time, ten seconds each (`rotate_seconds`): there is no way to use things on a wall screen yet, and numbered tabs in its header (with the event's name on a wide screen) say which one is in front.

**4. Any event can feed it through one record.** An event gives the board its id, kind (picks the emblem), name, start time, description, risks and an optional place (`IntelEvent`). The server's `IntelBoard` holds them; `NetServer.AnnounceIntel` and `WithdrawIntel` are the whole hook for the event lifecycle (a later ticket). The board travels whole in one event kind (185, `IntelSync`, protocol 28) at every change and to a joiner, with the time left rather than a clock time, so clients need no shared clock. The message also names the event being announced right now, if any. Until real events exist, an admin's `/event intel [clear]` puts a clearly marked test event on it, at the admin's own position.

**5. The announcement is text plus the village loudspeaker, not an NPC.** Players are told with a toast, the same line in the chat log, and the Rozhlas broadcast (which only the players near a pole hear). The text names the base's grid reference from the tactical map's sheet. It never says what the event is: that is the point of going to the base. An NPC that says it needs a model, animation and voice that do not exist yet; the toast covers everyone, and the loudspeaker is the in-world intercom the issue asks for.

Alternatives not built:
- The ground floor of a bunker: closest to the street, but small, with openings in the middle of every wall, so no wall is clear.
- All three command posts: three screens to keep in step and no one place to say "the base".
- A standing screen outdoors or a new building: needs a new placeable object with collision (ADR 0010) or a second kind of interior (ADR 0009 says no).
- A HUD panel opened with a key: not in-world, and the issue asks for a room. The screen's content is a plain Control, so it could be reused for one.

Consequences:
- The map must be exported again for the screen to appear (`bunkers.json` carries `intel`; no format bump, older loaders ignore it). Without it the base has no screen and the log says so.
- Three ladders down is a walk of some seconds; that is the cost of the command post. If it proves too far, the screen can move to a higher floor (a change in `_intel_screen` and `_underground` in `bunker.py`), at the price of the thematic fit: floor 1 is the crew quarters and floor 2 the ammunition store.
- Bots do not go in (ADR 0009) and do not read the screen.
